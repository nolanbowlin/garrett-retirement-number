# Correspondence, how to actually use these

Five formats for cold and early outreach to referral partners. Not for clients, not for
prospects who came in through the tool, and not for anyone already on the newsletter.

---

## Pick the format

| | Format | Use it for | Do not use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | `corr-01-letter` | **The default first touch.** Genuinely cold, one recipient at a time, sent from Garrett's own mailbox. | Anything going to more than a few people at once. |
| **B** | `corr-02-logo-band` | Someone who already knows him, or the same note to a handful of people at one firm. | A cold first touch. |
| **C** | `corr-03-credentials` | Attorneys, CPAs, valuation firms, bankers. Anyone whose own licence is exposed when they refer. | Business owners. To an owner this reads as a CV. |
| **D** | `corr-04-scenario` | CPAs and attorneys who see owner clients daily. Also the best second touch. | Anyone who will not recognise the scenario. |
| **E** | `corr-05-followup` | Touch two, five to seven business days after A. | A first contact. |

**The counterintuitive one is A.** It is the plainest template in the folder and it will
outperform the prettier ones on a first touch, because the recipient is deciding one thing
in two seconds: is this a person or is this a campaign. A masthead and a button answer
that the wrong way.

## The sequence that works

1. **Day 0.** Format A. One recipient, from Garrett's mailbox. Ask for a coffee, not a call.
2. **Day 5 to 7.** Format E. Under 60 words. Offer an easy no.
3. **Day 20 or so, optional.** Format D, if there is a genuine scenario worth telling.
4. **Stop.** Three touches is the limit. A professional who has not replied to three notes
   has answered.

A clean no from a referral partner is worth more than silence. It costs nothing and it
keeps the door open for a year from now, which is why E explicitly invites one.

## Who these are for

The partners worth the effort, roughly in order of how often they sit next to an owner
who is thinking about getting out:

- **CPAs.** The single highest value relationship. They see the whole picture and the
  owner already trusts them on money.
- **Business and M&A attorneys.** They are in the room when it turns real.
- **Business brokers.** Nolan is one, and the overlap is deliberate rather than awkward:
  the broker sells the business, Garrett handles what the owner personally needs it to
  produce. Different jobs, same client, neither competing.
- **Commercial bankers and lenders.** They know who is deleveraging ahead of a sale.
- **Property and casualty agents.** Adjacent, lower yield, but they know every owner in
  a trade.

## The compliance part, which is not optional

Garrett is a registered representative. Two separate regimes apply to a cold email and
they are easy to confuse with each other.

### FINRA Rule 2210, and the number 25

The rule sorts written communications by **how many retail investors receive them in a
rolling 30 calendar day period**:

- **25 or fewer** is *correspondence*. Subject to supervision and review, but generally
  not principal pre-approval.
- **More than 25** is a *retail communication*, which generally requires principal
  approval before first use.

Two things about that threshold that matter here:

1. **It counts recipients, not sends.** Emailing the same note to 30 different CPAs across
   three weeks is one retail communication, not 30 pieces of correspondence.
2. **"Retail investor" is defined by who the person is, not why you wrote to them.** It
   means anyone who is not an institutional investor, whether or not they have an account.
   A CPA you are asking for referrals plausibly still counts. Do not assume that writing
   to a professional about a business relationship takes the message outside the rule.

The practical read: keeping format A to genuinely small, hand sent batches is not just a
deliverability preference, it is what keeps these in the lighter category. **Confirm the
count and the classification with the OSJ before any campaign, because this is their call
and not ours.**

### CAN-SPAM

Applies to a single commercial email, not just to bulk. A cold prospecting email
advertising services is commercial, so every one of these needs:

- A physical postal address. Present in all five.
- A working opt-out, honoured within 10 business days.
- Accurate headers and a non-deceptive subject line.

Formats A and E use a reply based opt-out rather than a link, because a one to one letter
with an unsubscribe footer stops looking like a one to one letter. That is a defensible
mechanism as long as **somebody actually processes those replies**. If nobody will, or if
compliance prefers a link, switch them to the same footer B, C and D use. Do not simply
delete the line.

### The scenario template specifically

Format D describes a client situation. It must be **anonymised and true**. No names, no
industry specific enough to identify, no figures precise enough to trace. A fabricated
scenario is a fabricated client story, which is a compliance problem rather than a
copywriting shortcut. If there is no real pattern to describe, use a different format. The
template already carries a footnote saying the situation is illustrative and is not a
testimonial. Leave it there.

### Before any of this goes out

Every template carries `{{review_id}}`, waiting for the Guardian advertising review ID and
expiry in the format their own site uses, `8894350.1 Exp 5.2028`. Nothing sends without it.

## Writing the copy

- Under 150 words before the signature for A through D. Under 60 for E.
- One ask. For a referral partner the ask should almost always be a conversation, not a
  booking link and never a document.
- Say what Garrett does **not** do. "I do not do tax work, I do not do the deal, and I am
  not looking to move anyone away from the advisors they already have" removes the actual
  objection, which is that a stranger is going to poach a client.
- No adjectives in the credential strip. The moment it contains a claim it stops working.
- No em dashes, per house style.

The filled previews carry sample copy written for a CPA. Swap the specifics per partner
type; the structure holds.
